# MP2 Reflection — SafeWalk UW

## What did you build?

SafeWalk is a nighttime safety companion for University of Washington students walking home through the U-District after late classes or campus work. It has two parts: a Python data pipeline that pulls real Seattle Police Department crime records and OpenStreetMap street data to score every walkable corridor on a 0–100 safety scale, and a mobile-first web app (built with Lovable) that displays those scores on a Google Maps interface with walking directions, live GPS alerts, walking-buddy groups organized by major, Waze-style incident reporting, and one-tap emergency contacts for 911, UW SafeCampus, and Husky NightWalk. The pipeline computes a composite corridor score that combines crime density, street lighting, path type, and wheelchair accessibility, then writes the results as GeoJSON files that the app loads as a map overlay. A vanilla HTML/Leaflet prototype provides a lightweight fallback that uses the same data.

## What decisions did you make?

I chose Lovable as the frontend platform because it let me move quickly from UI concept to working prototype without deep React or deployment experience — I could focus on the safety logic and data pipeline rather than build tooling. For the data source, I used the Seattle Open Data Socrata API rather than a static CSV download so the pipeline can be re-run for fresh data. I scoped the crime filter to nighttime person-crimes (assault, robbery, rape/sex offenses, kidnapping) rather than all offenses because property crimes like car theft do not directly affect pedestrian safety. The corridor score formula weights crime density most heavily (60%) but also penalizes unlit streets, stairs, and rough surfaces because those factors compound risk at night. I built the demo GeoJSON as a lighter subset (segments with at least one data attribute) to keep the map responsive, since the full file is 19 MB and over 32,000 segments. I also added nine community hotspots sourced from r/udub posts as qualitative data that official records miss — places students actually warn each other about.

## What would you do differently?

First, I would turn reporting into a crowd-based safety layer: a live system that collects reports from users, lets other walkers add helpful votes, and allows SafeWalk volunteers to verify whether a report is real, outdated, or false. Right now every report is local to one device, which limits the tool's usefulness as a community safety resource. Second, I would add voice-based directions and voice-based safety alerts so users can walk hands-free, stay aware of their surroundings, and hear warnings when they are approaching an unfamiliar or higher-concern area instead of staring at their phones. I would also connect the app more directly with UW Police alerts and Husky NightWalk so serious incidents, such as assault reports, can be surfaced to nearby users faster and reduce reporting lag.
